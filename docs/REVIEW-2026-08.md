# ProManage NZ — application review

**Date:** 5 August 2026
**Scope:** performance, reliability, and quality assurance, assessed against the
plan to grow from 1 managed property to ~6.
**Commit reviewed:** `6b771e0`

---

## Summary

At 6 properties, **nothing in this app will be slow.** The production database
currently holds 1 property, 1 tenant, 3 inspections, 2 invoices, 27 owner
statements and 33 activity-log rows. Six properties does not change that
picture — not for IndexedDB, not for the renderers, not for Postgres.

The sync engine is the strongest part of the codebase and this review proposes
no changes to it. The incremental `updated_at` cursor, the rule that the cursor
only advances after a complete walk, the empty-list guard before the delete
pass, and `!local.synced → local wins` are all correct, and the reasoning
recorded in `CLAUDE.md` for each is sound.

The risks that matter are not performance risks. They are:

1. **There is no backup of anything, anywhere.** This is the single biggest
   risk in the project, and it is not a code-quality problem.
2. **Photos are captured at roughly six times the resolution anything in the
   app consumes**, which puts the project over the Supabase Free storage cap
   inside the first year at 6 properties.
3. **Nothing enforces the syntax check** that `CLAUDE.md` documents. This
   repository is the deploy root, so one unparseable edit to `index.html`
   publishes a blank application.

Findings are ordered by what they cost when they happen, not by how hard they
are to fix.

## Critical — data loss

### 1. No export path, and no backups behind it

The app can import (`importInvoiceJSON` at `index.html:5369`,
`importStatementJSON` at `index.html:5976`) but there is **no counterpart that
gets data out**. Neither is reachable from the UI; both are console helpers.

The Supabase organisation is on the **Free** plan, which has no automated
backups — Supabase's own documentation advises free-tier projects to export
regularly and keep copies off-platform.

So the complete set of copies of 27 owner statements — the agency's actual
books — is:

- one Free-tier Postgres database with no backup, and
- an IndexedDB store on a device, which the browser is permitted to evict.

A dropped table, a mistaken bulk delete, or a lost account ends the record.
Nothing in the current design recovers from that.

**Recommendation.** Add `exportAllData()` / `importAllData(file)` covering every
object store plus a schema version, surfaced on the Dashboard with a "last
exported" date that goes amber past 30 days. Register per the 8-point module
checklist in `CLAUDE.md`.

### 2. `navigator.storage.persist()` is never called

There is no occurrence of `storage.persist` or `storage.estimate` in
`index.html`. IndexedDB therefore sits in the browser's **best-effort** bucket
and is evictable under storage pressure — and the app actively creates that
pressure by storing 2.8 MB photos as base64 data URLs (≈3.8 MB each once
encoded).

An inspection recorded in a basement with no signal exists **only** in that
evictable store until it syncs.

**Recommendation.** Request persistent storage on first sign-in and show the
result, along with `navigator.storage.estimate()` usage, in the Backup card.

### 3. Quota exhaustion is unhandled

`dbPut` (`index.html:2354`) rejects with `QuotaExceededError` when the store is
full. `saveInspection` (`index.html:3371`) does not catch it — the form closes
and the user believes the inspection saved.

This is the failure most likely to be *caused* by finding 6 below.

### 4. Partial photo uploads are discarded

`uploadRecordPhotos` (`index.html:3682`) maps `Promise.all` over every area and
every photo within it. Consequences:

- **One failure rejects the whole batch.** The `catch` at `index.html:3731`
  returns `false`, and the storage refs for photos that *did* upload are thrown
  away. The next retry re-uploads all of them.
- **Every photo uploads concurrently.** Twenty photos × 2.8 MB simultaneously
  from a phone on mobile data at a property is the realistic case, and it is
  the case most likely to produce the failure above.

**Recommendation.** Upload sequentially, and write the record back to IndexedDB
after each successful photo so partial progress survives. Tolerate per-photo
failure and return what landed.

### 5. `openDB` has no `onblocked` handler

`openDB` (`index.html:2318`) registers `onupgradeneeded`, `onsuccess` and
`onerror` but not `onblocked`. Bumping `DB_VERSION` (currently 8, at
`index.html:2315`) while a second tab holds the old version leaves the promise
permanently unsettled.

The resulting failure looks exactly like the one `CLAUDE.md` warns about at
length: markup already on screen, function declarations hoisted, every handler
callable and nothing behind them working. There is no error in the console
because nothing threw — a promise simply never resolved.

The cached `dbInstance` also has no `onclose` recovery, so a connection closed
by the browser leaves every later call throwing `InvalidStateError`.

## High — cost and quota

### 6. Photos are ~6× larger than anything that consumes them

| Stage | Resolution | Quality | Size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Capture (`index.html:3265`) | 4032 × 3024 | 0.92 | ~2.8 MB |
| PDF report (`index.html:233`, `:340`) | max edge 1600 | 0.85 | ~200 KB |

Every captured pixel beyond 1600px on the long edge is decoded, base64-encoded,
written to IndexedDB, uploaded to Storage, re-downloaded at report time, and
then discarded by `normalizeToDataUrl`. The largest object currently in the
bucket is 2884 kB.

Projected at 6 properties — 2 inspections/year, 8 default areas, ~4 photos per
area:

| | Per year |
| --- | --- |
| As built | ~380 photos ≈ **1.1 GB** — over the 1 GB Free cap in year one |
| Downscaled at capture (1920px / q0.85) | ~380 photos ≈ **150 MB** |

1920px is deliberately above the report's 1600px cap, so **PDF output is
unchanged**. The crop-and-scale maths needed already exists and is proven, in
`FindingsReport.normalizeToDataUrl` (`index.html:269`–`341`) — lift `coverCrop`
and the canvas block into a shared helper rather than writing it twice.

A one-off console helper should re-encode the existing oversized objects so the
~11 MB already in the bucket comes down too.

### 7. Every PDF re-downloads the full-size originals

`normalizeToDataUrl` (`index.html:302`) fetches each photo at full size before
downscaling it. A 20-photo report pulls ~56 MB against a 5 GB uncached egress
allowance. Fixing finding 6 fixes this as a side effect.

### 8. Free projects auto-pause after 7 days of low activity

A property manager who opens the app weekly is precisely the usage pattern that
trips this. A paused project fails every sync; it is restorable within 90 days,
but the app gives no indication that this is what happened.

**Recommendation.** A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow issuing a trivial
PostgREST request a few times a week is sufficient to prevent it.

## Medium — reliability and correctness

### 9. Sync only runs at three moments

`fullSyncNow()` is called from `doLogin` (`index.html:2208`), the `online`
listener (`index.html:3769`), and six "Sync now" buttons. There is no interval
and no `visibilitychange` hook.

An installed PWA left open on a tablet never picks up edits made on the phone
until someone presses a button. With one device this is invisible; with a phone
in the field and a desktop in the office it is a stale-data bug waiting to
happen.

### 10. Sync failures retry forever, silently

The `syncPendingX` family (`index.html:3737` and its five siblings) has no
backoff, no attempt cap, and no way to surface *why* a push failed. The banner
reads "1 pending sync" indefinitely.

`schema.sql` §2 records this exact symptom as having already occurred once. The
mechanism is fixed; the diagnostic gap that made it hard to see is not.

### 11. Invoice and statement numbers can collide

`nextInvoiceNumber` (`index.html:5072`) reads local IndexedDB only, and the
schema has no unique constraint on the number. Two consequences:

- Two devices working offline both mint `INV-0005`.
- Once a `pendingDelete` invoice is purged from IndexedDB, its number is
  silently reused — two different invoices sharing one number, in GST records.

**Recommendation.** Add `unique (user_id, invoice_number)` and
`unique (user_id, statement_number)` in a new idempotent section of
`schema.sql`; on a 409, re-mint and retry once.

### 12. Money is held as raw floats

`subtotal * GST_RATE` (`index.html:5063`), the row sums in `saveStatement`
(`index.html:5639`–`5642`), and `opening + (income − expenses)`
(`index.html:5556`) all accumulate in binary floating point. Display rounds to
2 dp, so it looks correct — but the stored value can drift, and a PDF total can
differ from the sum of its own printed lines by a cent.

**Recommendation.** Round to cents at the boundary in `recalcInvoiceTotals`,
`recalcStatementTotals` and `saveStatement`.

### 13. Financial classification fails silently

`classifyStatementExpenseLine` and `categorizeExpenseDescription`
(`index.html:6035`–`6095`) match free-text descriptions against keyword lists
and fall through to `disbursement` when nothing matches.

A misclassified line **understates agency revenue**, and nothing reports that it
happened. More properties means more line-description variety, so the error rate
grows with exactly the change being planned.

**Recommendation.** Add an "Unreviewed lines" panel to `renderFinancials`
listing every line that reached the fallback without matching a keyword.

### 14. Dates are not validated on save

`parseFlexibleDate` returns `null` on an unparseable value and `addDaysToDateStr`
falls back to today (`index.html:4990`–`5004`). A typo'd issue date silently
becomes today's date on an invoice.

## Quality assurance

### 15. No CI, no tests, no hooks

There is no `.github/` directory. `CLAUDE.md` documents the two-block
`node --check` procedure as a ritual, but nothing runs it.

Because **this repository is the deploy root**, an unparseable `index.html`
reaching `main` publishes a blank application to GitHub Pages. The cost of the
gap is total and immediate; the cost of closing it is one workflow file.

**Recommended workflow**, on push and PR:

1. Extract both inline `<script>` blocks and `node --check` each.
2. Assert every `<script src>` in `index.html` exists on disk.
3. Assert `SHELL_FILES` in `sw.js` matches those paths — this drift is what
   produced the `Cannot access 'sb' before initialization` incident.
4. Decode `CONFIG.SUPABASE_KEY` and fail if `role === "service_role"`.
5. Load the page in headless Chromium; assert no uncaught errors and that the
   login gate renders.

## Reviewed and sound — no action recommended

- **The sync engine.** Incremental cursor, complete-walk rule, empty-list guard,
  `!local.synced` precedence. Correct as written.
- **Render volume.** `pullAllAndMerge` re-renders all ten modules
  (`index.html:2829`–`2838`), each issuing its own `dbGetAll`. At 6 properties
  this costs microseconds. Optimising it would trade real clarity for no
  measurable gain.
- **Security posture.** RLS is owner-scoped with one policy per command,
  `(select auth.uid())` is wrapped correctly, the publishable key belongs in the
  served file, the private bucket with `storage:` refs signed at display time is
  right, and `escapeHtml` / `escapeAttr` are applied consistently in the list
  renderers.
- **`sw.js`.** Every branch of the fetch handler resolves to a `Response`.
- **Supabase advisors.** One `WARN` — leaked-password protection, which requires
  the Pro plan and is already documented as a known open item — and ten
  `unused_index` INFO notices, which are the incremental-pull and foreign-key
  indexes on a database too small to have needed them yet. Both expected.

## Suggested order of work

Each phase is protected by the one before it.

| Phase | Work | Addresses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | CI workflow; scheduled keep-alive | 15, 8 |
| 1 | Export/import; `persist()`; quota handling; `onblocked`/`onclose` | 1, 2, 3, 5 |
| 2 | Downscale at capture; sequential upload with partial progress; re-encode existing objects | 6, 7, 4 |
| 3 | `visibilitychange` + interval sync; retry backoff with a visible reason | 9, 10 |
| 4 | Unique number constraints; cent rounding; unreviewed-lines panel; date validation | 11, 12, 13, 14 |

### On Free vs. Pro

Findings 1, 2 and 8 all trace back to the Free plan. Pro ($25/month) provides
daily backups with 7-day retention, no auto-pause, 100 GB storage, and the
leaked-password protection already noted as an open item.

Phase 1 and the keep-alive workflow make the Free plan genuinely workable, and
that is what this review assumes. But an in-app export is a safety net that
depends on somebody remembering to use it. Once the app holds a full year of
statements for 6 properties, an automated daily backup is worth more than the
engineering time already spent working around not having one.

## Verification for any of the above

There is no test runner. Minimum bar before any change ships:

```sh
python3 - <<'EOF'
import re
src = open('index.html', encoding='utf-8').read()
for i, b in enumerate(re.findall(r'<script(?![^>]*\bsrc=)[^>]*>(.*?)</script>', src, re.S)):
    open(f'/tmp/block{i}.js', 'w', encoding='utf-8').write(b)
EOF
node --check /tmp/block0.js && node --check /tmp/block1.js
```

Then, in a browser with DevTools open:

1. **Photos** — capture an inspection photo; confirm it lands at ~400 KB.
   Generate the PDF and compare against one produced before the change; it
   should be visually identical.
2. **Offline** — DevTools → Network → Offline. Save an inspection with photos,
   return online, confirm it syncs and the banner clears.
3. **Partial upload** — throttle to Slow 3G, kill the connection mid-upload,
   reconnect, and confirm already-uploaded photos are not re-sent.
4. **Export/import** — export, clear IndexedDB via Application → Storage, sign
   in, import, and confirm all 27 statements and both invoices return with
   correct totals.
5. **Two tabs** — open in two tabs and confirm a version bump prompts a reload
   rather than hanging.
6. **Integrity** — save an invoice with an existing number and confirm refusal;
   check a statement PDF total equals the sum of its lines to the cent.
7. **Advisors** — run `get_advisors` for security and performance after any DDL
   change, per `CLAUDE.md`.
